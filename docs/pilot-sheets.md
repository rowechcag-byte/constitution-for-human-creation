# Pilot sheets: Constitution for Human Creation

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Status:** Internal working drafts. Nobody has been approached to host either pilot.

Each pilot tests the eight Foundational Principles ([foundational-principles.md](foundational-principles.md), version 0.1) against one real AI use. A pilot counts only if a named human owner writes down a decision that the principles review changed.

---

## Pilot A: Organization already running an AI tool

**Host type:** One organization (a small business, nonprofit, or research group) that already uses an AI tool in its day-to-day work, such as a chatbot, a drafting assistant, a screening tool, or an agent. The tool has to be in real use, not in planning.

**Named owner:** One person at the host who has authority over how the tool is used and who signs the final decision. It can't be a committee or a vendor.

**Length:** About 30 days.

**Steps**, which borrow the Map and Measure steps of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework as a checklist, not as compliance:
1. **Map (week 1).** Write down what the tool does, who it affects, what goal a human set for it, and who can stop it.
2. **Measure (weeks 2–3).** Check the tool against the principles listed below. Collect evidence such as logs, sample outputs, and complaints. Note each place where the tool falls short.
3. **Decide (week 4).** The owner records one written decision.

**Principles it mainly tests:**
- 1: No Systematic Deception. Do people know when they're dealing with AI, and are outputs honest?
- 3: Humans Decide the Ends. Did a person set the goal, or did the tool drift into setting it?
- 4: Keep Systems Auditable. If something went wrong, could anyone trace it?
- 6: Named Human Responsibility. Is there a real person who answers for it?
- The others (2, 5, 7, 8) are checked briefly and flagged if relevant.

**What counts as a changed decision.** The owner does at least one of these because of the review, and says so in writing:
- changes how the tool is used, for example by adding disclosure, a human check, or logging
- pauses or narrows the tool
- stops using it, or decides not to expand it
- goes ahead unchanged, but with a written reason tied to the principles. This counts as a weaker result and gets reported as one.

**Output:** A one-page case note covering what was found, what was decided, and who decided. It goes in the adopter/pilot register. The note gets a review date 6 months out.

## Pilot B: Public service desk offering an AI service to residents

**Host type:** One public-facing civic desk, such as a library, city or county office, or community service point, that offers or is about to offer one AI-assisted service to the public. Examples are a help chatbot, an AI search or reference aid, or a form assistant.

**Named owner:** One staff member who is responsible for that service. There is also at least one **public reviewer** who uses the service but doesn't work on it, which gives the pilot the public side of the hybrid model.

**Length:** About 30–45 days.

**Steps:**
1. **Map.** Describe the service, who uses it, what it may and may not do, and who can switch it off.
2. **Measure.** The owner checks it against the principles. Separately, the public reviewer uses the service and writes down what felt unclear, unfair, or wrong.
3. **Decide.** The owner records a decision. The public reviewer's notes are attached, whether or not the owner agrees with them.

**Principles it mainly tests:**
- 1: No Systematic Deception. Is it clear to residents that it's AI, and what its limits are?
- 5: Digital Does Not Override Physical. Can residents still reach a person and a non-digital path?
- 6: Named Human Responsibility. Who answers when it's wrong?
- 7: Limits on Coercion and Surveillance. What does it collect or keep about residents?
- 8: Protect Human Creative Agency. Does it help people find and learn, or just steer them?

**What counts as a changed decision.** The owner does at least one of these because of the review:
- adds or improves disclosure, a human fallback, or data limits
- narrows what the service does or who it's offered to
- delays or declines launch, or retires the service
- proceeds unchanged, with a written reason that answers the public reviewer's notes. This counts as a weaker result.

**Output:** A one-page case note with the owner's decision and the public reviewer's notes. It goes in the register, gets a review date, and a plain-language version can be published once the host agrees.

## Rules for both pilots

- Before any host is approached, the approach needs the initiative's go-ahead.
- The host decides what can be published. Nothing about a host goes public without its written OK.
- The pilot is a review, not a certification. Hosts may not claim they are "certified" or "compliant" with Constitution for Human Creation.
- If a pilot finds that a principle is unworkable or unclear, that gets logged as input for amending the principles.
